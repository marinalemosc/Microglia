Microglial Morphological Analysis in Aged Mice and Growth Hormone Treatment
Project Overview

This project focuses on the histomorphometric analysis of microglia to evaluate their inflammatory activation profile in aged mice and in aged mice treated with growth hormone (GH). 

Microglia, the primary mediators of neuroinflammatory processes, exhibit dynamic morphophysiological profiles depending on their microenvironmental signaling. During aging, there is an increase in circulating pro-inflammatory cytokines and hormonal alterations, including a decline in growth hormone production, which is known to exert trophic effects throughout the body. In the central nervous system, aging promotes neuroinflammation, contributing to the development of neurodegenerative diseases. Vigilant microglia exhibit a small, highly branched cell body and secrete trophic factors, whereas activated microglia display a pro-inflammatory phenotype with an amoeboid morphology and reduced ramification. Aging is associated with a dysregulated pro-inflammatory microglial phenotype.

Objectives

The main objective of this study is to analyze morphological alterations in microglia from:

Young mice (2 months old)

Aged mice (16–18 months old)

Aged mice treated with growth hormone

Methodology

Microglial cells were identified in histological brain sections using immunofluorescence with an anti-Iba1 antibody. High-resolution photomicrographs of the stained sections were acquired and subjected to histomorphometric analysis using ImageJ with the Skeleton and Fraclac plugins. Subsequent analyses will involve machine learning techniques to cluster microglial cells based on their morphological profiles.


Expected Outcomes

This study aims to provide quantitative insights into microglial morphological changes associated with aging and to investigate the modulatory effects of growth hormone treatment on microglial activation. Ultimately, these analyses may contribute to a better understanding of neuroinflammatory mechanisms underlying age-related neurodegeneration.
